South American Players With the Most Premier League Appearances for Every Big Six Club

By Club Ten · Published 29 August 2026 · CONMEBOL nations only · data through end of 2025/26

South America has produced some of the most electrifying players in Premier League history. Argentina and Brazil lead the way, dominating the lists of South American appearance-makers across the Big Six. Here are the ten South American players with the most Premier League appearances for each of the Big Six clubs.

This list overlaps with Club Ten's 19 August daily puzzles across all six clubs — spoiler warning if you haven't played those yet.

Arsenal's South American players with the most Premier League appearances

Arsenal's South American contingent is almost entirely Brazilian, topped by Gabriel Martinelli and Gabriel Magalhães — two pillars of Mikel Arteta's title-challenging sides. Chile's Alexis Sánchez, fourth with 122 appearances, remains one of the most gifted South Americans to play for the club. Gilberto Silva, who anchored the Invincibles midfield, sits third.

Ederson and Fernandinho lead the Brazilians; Sergio Agüero and Pablo Zabaleta are Argentina's contribution, with Zabaleta's 230 appearances across nine seasons making him one of the most durable outfield players the club has had. Carlos Tevez and Julián Álvarez — two very different generations of Argentine forwards — both feature.

Tottenham's South American players with the most Premier League appearances

Cristian Romero has become a key figure in Tottenham's defence. Who leads each Big Six club?

Gabriel Martinelli leads Arsenal, Willian leads Chelsea, Roberto Firmino leads Liverpool, Ederson leads Manchester City, Antonio Valencia leads Manchester United, and Erik Lamela leads Tottenham.

Brazil's outsized share

Add up all sixty slots across these six lists and the split is stark: 35 are Brazilian, 16 Argentine, and just 9 spread across every other South American nation combined — Uruguay, Colombia, Chile, Ecuador and the rest sharing what's left. Arsenal's list is nine-tenths Brazilian. Liverpool's is six of ten. Only Manchester City comes close to an even split between the continent's two footballing giants. Between them, Brazil and Argentina account for 51 of the 60 places across the six lists — an extraordinary 85%, from a continent of ten countries.

The smaller nations that broke through anyway

Ecuador supplies just two names across all sixty slots. Antonio Valencia leads Manchester United's entire South American contingent outright, while Moisés Caicedo ranks seventh at Chelsea — a smaller share of the list, but still a real presence at a club where Brazilian and Argentine players otherwise dominate. Uruguay does something similar in miniature: Luis Suárez sits sixth at Liverpool and Rodrigo Bentancur sixth at Tottenham, while Diego Forlán and Gus Poyet round out the bottom of United's and Spurs' lists respectively — a small but persistent Uruguayan footprint despite the country supplying a fraction of the volume Brazil and Argentina do.

Methodology & sources

Nationality is based on senior international representation, not birthplace — a player born in a CONMEBOL country who represented a different nation at senior level isn't counted here. Ranked by total Premier League appearances (starts and substitute appearances combined) for the club in question. Players level on appearances are shown as tied. Figures are cross-checked against Wikipedia's season-by-season appearance tables, Premier League's official player statistics pages, and each club's official historical records before publication. Data runs through the end of the 2025/26 season and does not reflect the 2026 summer transfer window.
