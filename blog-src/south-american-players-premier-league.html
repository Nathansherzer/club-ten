South American Players in the Premier League: The Greatest at Arsenal, Chelsea, Liverpool, Man City, Man United and Spurs

By Club Ten Research Team · Published 6 September 2026 · CONMEBOL nations only · data through end of 2025/26

South America has produced some of the most electrifying players in Premier League history. Argentina and Brazil lead the way — the two nations alone account for the vast majority of CONMEBOL appearances in the English top flight. Here are the ten most-capped South Americans at each of the Big Six clubs.

This list overlaps with Club Ten's 19 August daily puzzles across all six clubs — spoiler warning if you haven't played those yet.

Arsenal

Arsenal's South American contingent is almost entirely Brazilian, topped by Gabriel Magalhães and Gabriel Martinelli — two pillars of Mikel Arteta's title-challenging sides. Chile's Alexis Sánchez, fourth with 122 appearances, remains one of the most gifted South Americans to play for the club. Gilberto Silva, who anchored the Invincibles midfield, sits third.

Liverpool

Liverpool's South American list is led by Roberto Firmino — the Brazilian who became one of the most beloved players in the club's modern era despite never being the obvious star. Luis Suárez (Uruguay) sits sixth, a reminder of one of the most compelling and controversial players the Premier League has ever seen. Colombia's Luis Díaz and Argentina's Alexis Mac Allister and Javier Mascherano broaden the mix.

Manchester City

Manchester City's South American list is split evenly between Brazil and Argentina — the two nations who have defined City's dominance. Ederson and Fernandinho lead the Brazilians; Sergio Agüero and Pablo Zabaleta are Argentina's contribution. Carlos Tevez and Julián Álvarez — two very different generations of Argentine forwards — both feature.

Manchester United

Antonio Valencia of Ecuador leads United's South American list — a converted winger who became one of the club's most reliable right-backs. The Brazilian contingent of Fred, Casemiro, Rafael and Anderson accounts for the bulk of appearances. Argentina's Alejandro Garnacho, Lisandro Martínez and Marcos Rojo represent a different era of South American input at Old Trafford.

Tottenham

Spurs' South American list is headlined by Argentina's Erik Lamela — a cult figure at White Hart Lane whose rabona goal against Arsenal in the League Cup remains one of the most audacious in Premier League history. Cristian Romero has become one of the best defenders in the league. Colombia's Davinson Sánchez and Argentina's Mauricio Taricco, a full-back from the club's late-1990s sides, round out a list that spans three different decades.

Brazil's outsized share

Add up all sixty slots across these six lists and the split is stark: 35 are Brazilian, 16 Argentine, and just 9 spread across every other South American nation combined — Uruguay, Colombia, Chile, Ecuador and the rest sharing what's left. Arsenal's list is nine-tenths Brazilian. Liverpool's is seven of ten. Only Manchester City comes close to an even split between the continent's two footballing giants. For a continent of ten countries, two nations are doing almost all of the exporting to the Big Six.

The smaller nations that broke through anyway

Ecuador supplies just two names across all sixty slots — but both lead their club's entire South American contingent outright. Antonio Valencia tops Manchester United's list, and Moisés Caicedo is high on Chelsea's, at clubs where Brazilian and Argentine players otherwise dominate every other position. Uruguay does something similar in miniature: Luis Suárez, Diego Forlán, Rodrigo Bentancur and Gus Poyet all rank prominently despite Uruguay supplying a fraction of the total volume Brazil and Argentina do. Representation from the smaller CONMEBOL nations is thin, but where it exists, it tends to matter.

Methodology & sources

Nationality is based on senior international representation, not birthplace — a player born in a CONMEBOL country who represented a different nation at senior level isn't counted here. Ranked by total Premier League appearances (starts and substitute appearances combined) for the club in question. Figures are cross-checked against each club's official historical records and independent match databases before publication. Data runs through the end of the 2025/26 season and does not reflect the 2026 summer transfer window.
