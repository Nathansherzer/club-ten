Ten Managers, One History Exam: Arsenal's Permanent Bosses Since 1958

By Club Ten · Published 12 November 2026

Name your club's ten most recent permanent managers. Sounds simple. At Arsenal, it's a history exam.

Work backwards from Mikel Arteta: Emery, Wenger, Rioch, Graham, Howe, Neill, Mee, Wright. Nine names in, and you're at Billy Wright, appointed in 1962.

Before Pelé's first World Cup

The tenth name is George Swindin. Before managing Arsenal, he had made 297 appearances in their goal. He took the manager's job on 21 June 1958, during that summer's World Cup in Sweden. Eight days later, a 17-year-old Pelé scored twice in the final.

When Arsenal's tenth most recent manager got the job, Brazil had never won a World Cup.

The careers hidden inside ten names

But the distance back to 1958 is only half the story. Look at the careers hidden inside those ten names.

Billy Wright became the first player to reach 100 England caps, but his reputation as a player didn't translate into success at Highbury. Arsenal never finished higher than seventh under him.

His replacement wasn't another famous international. It was the club physiotherapist.

From the treatment room to the Double

Bertie Mee had spent six years treating Arsenal players before the board handed him the team in 1966. Five years later, he won the Double. Arsenal clinched the league at White Hart Lane, then beat Liverpool in the FA Cup final. From the treatment room to a league title at Tottenham's ground: that's a career change worth remembering.

Terry Neill arrived from Tottenham to become Arsenal's youngest manager and took the club to three consecutive FA Cup finals. Don Howe's spell brought no major trophy, but his work developing players including Tony Adams, David Rocastle and Niall Quinn belongs in the story too. A manager's contribution doesn't always fit neatly into the honours column.

Anfield '89, and a reign that lasted 61 weeks

George Graham's does. The league won at Anfield on the final night in 1989. Another title two years later. These aren't just names to fill quiz slots; they're the people attached to stories supporters pass down.

Then there's Bruce Rioch, who lasted only 61 weeks. A brief appointment, but not an insignificant one: Dennis Bergkamp arrived from Inter during his tenure. Even a short reign can leave something that lasts.

22 seasons, then just two more names

And then came the biggest single stretch: Arsène Wenger's 22 seasons. Three league titles, seven FA Cups, an unbeaten league season and the move to a new stadium. Since Wenger, only Emery and Arteta have held the job permanently. Someone who started following Arsenal during Wenger's reign has watched just three permanent managers.

Caretakers don't count. Freddie Ljungberg won't get you another slot, and neither will Pat Rice or Stewart Houston. That's how ten names reach across 68 years.

Same question, different amount of history

For comparison, Chelsea's tenth most recent permanent manager is José Mourinho, appointed for his second spell in June 2013. Their list reaches back 13 years. Arsenal's begins before Pelé's first World Cup win.

Ten sounds like the same amount of history for everyone. It isn't. At one club, it's a crowded stretch of modern football. At Arsenal, it's several generations of supporters: a former goalkeeper, an England legend, a physiotherapist, title winners and one appointment that lasted barely a year.

Any Arsenal fan who typed "Swindin": take a bow. You weren't just remembering managers. You were doing family history. Think you know your own club's list? Play today's Arsenal puzzle or today's Chelsea puzzle and find out.

Methodology & sources

Permanent managers only; repeat spells are counted once, at the most recent spell. Caretaker appointments are excluded. Arsenal's and Chelsea's lists are checked to November 2026.

Sources: Arsenal's official manager profiles, the club's account of Bertie Mee's appointment, contemporaneous reporting on Bruce Rioch signing Dennis Bergkamp, and Wolverhampton Wanderers' profile of Billy Wright.
