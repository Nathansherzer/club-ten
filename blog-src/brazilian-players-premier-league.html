Brazilian Players in the Premier League: Appearance Leaders at Every Big Six Club

By Club Ten · Published 17 August 2026 · Updated 28 August 2026 · Data through end of 2025/26 season

Brazil has sent a steady stream of technically gifted players to the Premier League. From Gilberto Silva's invincible season at Arsenal to Ederson redefining the goalkeeper position at Manchester City, Brazilians have played a significant role in the competition's recent history. Below are ranked top-10 appearance tables for Arsenal, Chelsea, Manchester City and Manchester United; Liverpool and Tottenham are covered with a shorter summary instead, since neither club has had enough Brazilian players to build a reliable ten-deep ranking.

Arsenal

Arsenal's Brazilian connection runs deep. Gilberto Silva anchored the Invincibles midfield in 2003/04, while Gabriel Magalhães and Gabriel Martinelli are now among the most important players at the club — with both already in the top two for all-time Brazilian appearances at Arsenal despite still being in their mid-twenties.

Chelsea

Among the top-10 tables below, Chelsea's combined total is the highest of the four clubs with full rankings. Willian leads with 234 — the most by a Brazilian at Chelsea, though Manchester City's Ederson (277) and Fernandinho (264) both have more across the Big Six — ahead of David Luiz and Ramires. Thiago Silva's 113 appearances at the age he joined the club make him one of the most remarkable Brazilian signings in Premier League history. Estêvão, at just 18, is already building a legacy.

Liverpool

Liverpool's Brazilian contingent has been relatively small but packed with quality. Roberto Firmino's 256 Premier League appearances made him the cornerstone of Klopp's title-winning side. Alisson Becker has established himself as one of the greatest goalkeepers in the club's history. Fabinho and Philippe Coutinho round out a group of Brazilians who all played significant roles at Anfield.

Manchester City

Manchester City's Brazilian spine has been the foundation of their Premier League dominance. Ederson leads with 277 appearances and has redefined what a goalkeeper can offer — his passing ability as much a weapon as his shot-stopping. Fernandinho, 13 appearances behind at 264, spent a decade as City's engine room. Together they form arguably the greatest Brazilian duo in Premier League history.

Manchester United

United's Brazilian history is headlined by Fred, Casemiro, Rafael and Anderson — four very different players who between them covered 464 Premier League appearances in red. Casemiro's impact on United's midfield was immediate and transformative. Matheus Cunha, arriving from Wolves in 2025, is the latest Brazilian to make the move to Old Trafford.

Tottenham

Spurs have had fewer Brazilians than most of their rivals, but Lucas Moura's dramatic semi-final hat-trick against Ajax in 2019 gave them one of the most memorable Brazilian moments in Premier League club football. Richarlison and Sandro have also left their mark at the club.

Goalkeepers, not just flair players

The stereotype of the Brazilian import is a tricky forward or a box-to-box midfielder, but two of the Big Six's most important Brazilians play in goal. Ederson's 277 appearances for Manchester City and Alisson Becker's record at Liverpool both rest on a skill set that barely existed in English football before them — a goalkeeper who's also the first line of a possession-based build-up. Both arrived within a year of each other, at the two clubs that went on to dominate the following decade — a case study in how one nation's goalkeeping style reshaped a position's requirements at the top of the English game.

One star versus a whole lineage

Compare how concentrated each club's Brazilian history is. Chelsea and Manchester City both lean on one dominant figure — Willian's 234 appearances are 74 more than David Luiz, Chelsea's next-most-capped Brazilian, and Ederson alone accounts for well over a quarter of City's Brazilian appearance total. Arsenal tells a different story: no single player dominates, and the top three (Gabriel Magalhães, Gabriel Martinelli, Gilberto Silva) are separated by fewer than 25 appearances despite representing three different Arsenal eras, a decade apart from each other. That's less a single great Brazilian at Arsenal and more an unbroken chain of them.

Still writing their chapter

Several names on these lists are far from finished. Gabriel Martinelli, already second on Arsenal's list at 191 appearances, is still in his mid-twenties. Estêvão has played only 22 games for Chelsea so far — the club's history with him has barely started. Matheus Cunha's 33 appearances at Manchester United came almost entirely in a single season since his move from Wolves. Revisit this list in three or four seasons and several of today's rankings will have moved.

Methodology & sources

Appearance totals count all Premier League matches (starts and substitute appearances combined) for the club in question only — spells at other Premier League clubs aren't included in a player's total here. Nationality is based on the player's senior international representation; a Brazilian-born player who represented a different country at senior level isn't counted as Brazilian for this list. Figures are cross-checked against Wikipedia's season-by-season appearance tables, Premier League's official player statistics pages, and each club's official historical squad records before publication. Liverpool and Tottenham are covered without full ranked tables, reflecting the smaller number of Brazilian players who've featured prominently enough at those two clubs to build a reliable top-10. Data runs through the end of the 2025/26 season and does not reflect the 2026 summer transfer window.
