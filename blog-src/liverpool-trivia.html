Liverpool Trivia: The Miracle of Istanbul, From 3-0 Down to Champions

By Club Ten · Published 5 September 2026

On 25 May 2005, Liverpool were 3-0 down to AC Milan at half-time in the Champions League final and won the trophy anyway, a comeback now known simply as the Miracle of Istanbul. They scored three goals in six second-half minutes, drew it level at 3-3, and won it on penalties in the early hours of the following morning. It's the single most repeated fact in the club's history, and for good reason. What tends to get skipped is exactly how those six minutes happened, and what nearly happened to Liverpool's own captain six weeks later.

Three down, and it wasn't close

This Milan side is still argued over as one of the best club teams Europe has produced: Maldini, Nesta and Cafu at the back, Pirlo and Gattuso in midfield, Kaká and Shevchenko further forward. They led inside a minute, Paolo Maldini scoring off an Andrea Pirlo free kick just 52 seconds in, still the fastest goal in Champions League final history two decades on. Hernán Crespo doubled the lead in the 39th minute, then chipped in a third in the 44th to make it 3-0 at the break. 3-0 down to arguably the best team in Europe, at half-time, in a European final. Nobody expected what came next.

Six minutes

Rafael Benítez turned to Dietmar Hamann at half-time, sending him on for the injured Steve Finnan, shifting to a back three and pushing Steven Gerrard further forward. It worked almost immediately. Gerrard headed in a cross in the 54th minute, Vladimír Šmicer struck from distance two minutes later to make it 3-2, and in the 60th minute Xabi Alonso had a penalty saved by Dida, only to score the rebound himself. Three goals, six minutes, 3-3. Milan had gone from cruising to shell-shocked in less time than it takes to make a cup of tea.

Spaghetti legs

Extra time nearly undid the comeback before it counted for anything, Jerzy Dudek produced a double save to deny Shevchenko in the second period, one of the finest moments of goalkeeping the competition has seen. It went to penalties. As the shootout began, Jamie Carragher grabbed Dudek and told him to copy Bruce Grobbelaar's "wobbly legs" routine from Liverpool's 1984 European Cup final win, shouting "Spaghetti, Grobbelaar, '84!" at him on the line. Dudek did it. Milan missed three of their five penalties: Serginho blazed his opening kick over the bar, and Dudek himself saved from both Andrea Pirlo and, finally, Shevchenko, sealing a 3-2 shootout win for Liverpool after trailing 3-0 at half-time.

The captain nearly wasn't there the following season

Here's the part that gets left out. Just six weeks later, on 5 July 2005, Steven Gerrard, the captain whose header started the comeback, handed in a transfer request amid a stalled contract standoff with Liverpool. Chelsea, freshly crowned champions under José Mourinho, had a £32 million bid ready. Gerrard withdrew the request the very next day and signed a new contract instead, but for about 24 hours that summer, the man who started the Miracle of Istanbul very nearly wasn't wearing a Liverpool shirt again.

The Miracle of Istanbul didn't end at the final whistle

Liverpool have won six European Cups in total, more than any other English club, but this is the one people actually talk about, not because of the trophy itself, but because of how implausible the route there was. Three goals down to one of the great continental sides, level within six minutes, sealed by a wobbly-legged goalkeeper, and won by a captain whose header started the comeback and who nearly left the club six weeks later.
