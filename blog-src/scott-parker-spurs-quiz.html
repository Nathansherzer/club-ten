Only 6% Found Scott Parker in Our Spurs PFA Quiz

By Club Ten · Stories Behind the Answers · Spurs Puzzle #114 · Published 18 November 2026

Harry Kane was found by 99% of recorded starters. Gareth Bale was found by 81%. But Scott Parker, who joined Bale and Kyle Walker in the 2011/12 PFA Premier League Team of the Year, was found by just 6%.

That made Parker the least-found answer in our Tottenham puzzle by a remarkable margin. Even the next least-found player, Danny Rose, was found by 48% of starters, about eight times Parker's rate.

There is an extra twist to the timing. On 4 November 2026, one day before the puzzle's date, West Ham United appointed Parker as head coach. A player many of our recorded starters did not find was suddenly making football headlines again.

What was Spurs Puzzle #114 asking?

The question asked players to name the ten most recent different players selected for the PFA Premier League Team of the Year while playing for Tottenham Hotspur.

Kane, Son Heung-min, Dele Alli and Christian Eriksen were among the answers. So were Bale and Walker. Parker was the tenth name on the list because his last qualifying selection was in 2011/12, earlier than any of the other nine players' last selections.

He was not an obscure award winner. Spurs themselves confirmed that Parker, Bale and Walker all made the PFA's Premier League Team of the Year in April 2012. Walker also won the PFA Young Player of the Year award. Source: Tottenham Hotspur.

That result is striking because Parker was selected in the same 2011/12 PFA team as Walker and Bale, yet those two were found much more often.

The data tells us what was entered correctly, not why. Parker's shorter spell at Tottenham and the age of his selection may have played a part, but we cannot establish that from these results alone.

Why Scott Parker's Tottenham selection matters

Parker arrived at Spurs in August 2011 with an unusual distinction. He had just won the Football Writers' Association Footballer of the Year award for 2010/11, despite West Ham being relegated that season. Earlier, in 2003/04, he had won the PFA Young Player of the Year award while at Chelsea.

At Tottenham, Parker brought energy and defensive work to a midfield that included Luka Modrić and Rafael van der Vaart. His first season was strong enough for fellow professionals to select him for the 2011/12 Premier League Team of the Year, alongside teammates Bale and Walker.

His Spurs career was relatively short. He made 50 Premier League appearances for Tottenham before leaving for Fulham in 2013. That makes the recognition especially notable: a player with only 50 Spurs league appearances still earned a place among the league's best in 2011/12.

From Tottenham midfielder to West Ham head coach

After retiring as a player, Parker coached Tottenham's under-18s before moving into first-team coaching and management. He went on to guide Fulham, Bournemouth and Burnley to promotion from the Championship.

His Burnley side reached 100 points in the 2024/25 Championship season and conceded only 16 league goals, according to West Ham's account of his managerial career.

Then came his return to West Ham on 4 November 2026. Fifteen years after leaving the club as its reigning FWA Footballer of the Year, Parker was appointed to lead its push back towards the Premier League.

That is what makes his appearance in our puzzle such a good football story. The least-found name on the Spurs list belongs to a player who won major individual awards, earned a place in a PFA Team of the Year and is now back in charge at one of his former clubs.

How we measured the answers

Percentages show the share of GA4 active users who started the Spurs puzzle dated 5 November 2026 and then recorded a correct guess for each answer, within 4–6 November in the New York reporting timezone. A start means submitting the first guess. Each answer is measured independently against the same group of recorded starters, so the percentages are not meant to add up to 100%.

Figures are rounded. GA4 reporting identities do not necessarily represent individual people, and these results are not a survey of Spurs fans. Puzzle days begin at midnight UK time, which falls on the previous evening in New York. Later plays and incorrect guesses are not included. The data shows which answers were entered correctly, not what someone remembered but did not enter.
