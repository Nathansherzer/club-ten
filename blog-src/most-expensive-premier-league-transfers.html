The Most Expensive Premier League-to-Premier League Transfers of All Time

By Club Ten · Published 22 August 2026 · Updated 28 August 2026 · Fees in euros · through 2025/26 (excludes 2026 summer window)

The Premier League's spending power has turned domestic transfers into nine-figure deals. Fees below are shown in euros, the currency most transfer databases report in, even though these are deals between two English clubs — see the methodology note for how figures were sourced. Here are the biggest PL-to-PL transfers ever recorded, club by club.

Note: several of the fees below match the answers to Club Ten's 20 August daily puzzles across all six clubs — if you haven't played those yet, you may want to do that first.

The biggest deal isn't the biggest spend

Liverpool hold the single record fee, but add up each club's five biggest PL-to-PL purchases and a different picture appears. Manchester United's top five (Maguire, Lukaku, Mbeumo, Cunha, Mount) total €388.60m — the highest of the six. Manchester City (€383m) and Chelsea (€381.60m) are close behind, with Arsenal (€375.40m) not far off that group either. Liverpool's combined total, at €367.75m, actually ranks fifth of six, despite owning the single record fee. Spending big once and spending big consistently turn out to be two different skills — Tottenham, with the lowest combined total by a clear margin at €287.40m, have generally done neither.

The Bournemouth effect

One selling club shows up three times across these tables at meaningfully high fees: Bournemouth, who sold Milos Kerkez to Liverpool (€46.90m), Antoine Semenyo to Manchester City (€72m), and Dominic Solanke to Tottenham (€64.30m). For a club that spent most of the 2010s and early 2020s as a Premier League survival story, becoming a three-time supplier to Big Six spending sprees in the same window is a notable shift — the kind of thing that tends to get missed when attention stays fixed on the buying club's record fee rather than who's selling.

Methodology & sources

Fees cover confirmed transfers directly between two Premier League clubs only — a player signed from a club outside the Premier League at the time of transfer isn't counted, even if that club was promoted or relegated around the same window. Reported fees can vary between sources when add-ons and bonuses are involved; figures here reflect the base transfer fee as most widely reported, cross-checked against Transfermarkt, contemporaneous reporting from major outlets (BBC Sport, Sky Sports, ESPN), and official club transfer announcements before publication. Data runs through the end of the 2025/26 season and does not include the 2026 summer transfer window.
