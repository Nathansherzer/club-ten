Manchester City Trivia: The Only Champions Ever Relegated

By Club Ten · Published 7 September 2026

Manchester City are the only club in English history to win the top-flight title and be relegated the very next season. They managed it in 1938, spent 1998 in the third tier, and then in 2012 produced the most dramatic title-winning moment the English game has ever seen. No club swings between disaster and glory quite like City, and once you know the full arc, the old phrase "typical City" makes complete sense.

The champions who got relegated

In 1936-37, City won the First Division for the first time in their history. The next season, 1937-38, they were relegated, still the only time the reigning English champions have gone straight down. The truly City part of it: they were the highest-scoring team in the entire division that season. Peter Doherty scored 23 league goals and they still went down. Being relegated as champions while outscoring everyone else in the division is the kind of thing that only happens to one club, and it happened to this one. City fans have long traced the phrase "typical City" back to exactly this.

All the way to the third tier

It got worse before it got better. In 1996 City were relegated from the Premier League, and on 3 May 1998 they went down again, into the third tier of English football for the first and only time in their history. A club that had been champions of England six decades earlier was now two divisions below the top, sharing a league with sides it had no business being anywhere near.

93:20

Then, on 13 May 2012, the swing went all the way back up. City needed to beat QPR on the final day to win their first league title in 44 years, and to do it on goal difference ahead of Manchester United, who had already won their own game and were celebrating as champions. With QPR down to ten men after a Joey Barton red card, City were somehow losing 2-1 deep into stoppage time. Edin Dzeko headed an equaliser in the 92nd minute. Then, with the clock reading 93 minutes and 20 seconds, Sergio Aguero collected a pass from Mario Balotelli, took a touch, and smashed it in. City were champions. It remains the only time in Premier League history the title has been settled on goal difference on the final day.

The kicker

Put the arc together and it is almost absurd: the only English champions ever relegated the following season, the only top-flight team relegated as the division's leading scorers, a spell in the third tier, and then the single most replayed final-day moment in Premier League history. "Typical City" used to be shorthand for finding a new way to fall over. For a long time now it has meant something much closer to the opposite. Think you know the rest of the City story?
