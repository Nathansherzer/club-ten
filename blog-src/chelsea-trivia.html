Chelsea Trivia: Founded By Accident, Won By Chaos

By Club Ten · Published 4 September 2026

Chelsea Football Club exists because Fulham turned down a stadium lease. Most clubs form first and go looking for a ground afterwards. Chelsea happened the other way round, and once you know that, the club's biggest ever trophy starts to make a strange kind of sense too.

A club invented to fill a stadium

Gus Mears and his brother bought the Stamford Bridge athletics ground in 1904 and wanted to lease it to an existing local club. He approached Fulham FC. The deal fell through. Rather than find another tenant, Mears just built one, forming an entirely new club to fill the stadium he already owned. It was founded on 10 March 1905 in a pub across the road, the Rising Sun, now called the Butcher's Hook. They couldn't call it Fulham FC, since the actual Fulham was right there in the same part of London, so the founders considered names like Stamford Bridge FC, Kensington FC and London FC before settling on Chelsea, after the neighbouring borough.

Decades pass, then everything changes overnight

Chelsea spent most of the 20th century as an inconsistent club, not a mediocre one. There was a shock League Championship in 1955, after years of finishing in the bottom half. A first FA Cup in 1970, beating Leeds United in a bruising replay at Old Trafford. A first European trophy a year later, the Cup Winners' Cup, beating Real Madrid in a replay in Athens. Real peaks, mixed in with spells outside the top flight entirely. Then in 2003 Roman Abramovich bought the club and turned it into one of the best-funded sides in world football almost overnight, a genuine step change rather than another swing of the same pendulum. What most casual fans don't clock is that even with that investment rebuilding the squad, it took Chelsea nine more years to win the trophy that mattered most, and the way it happened had more to do with resilience than dominance.

Sacked in March, champions of Europe by May

André Villas-Boas was fired as Chelsea manager on 4 March 2012, just eight months into the job, a day after a 1-0 defeat at West Brom left the club fifth in the league. Roberto Di Matteo, previously a Chelsea player and part of the coaching staff, took over as caretaker that same day with no long-term security and nothing to lose. Just over two months later, on 5 May, he'd won the FA Cup, beating Liverpool 2-1 at Wembley. Two weeks after that, he'd won the Champions League.

Bayern's own back yard

The 2012 final was played at Bayern Munich's own stadium, the Allianz Arena, the first Champions League final held at one finalist's home ground since Roma hosted, and lost, the 1984 European Cup final at their own Stadio Olimpico. Bavarian fans had a name for it beforehand: Finale Dahoam, "the final at home." Bayern dominated for long stretches without finding a breakthrough, until Thomas Müller finally scored in the 83rd minute. Five minutes later, Didier Drogba headed in an equaliser to force extra time, and Chelsea went on to win 4-3 on penalties, with Drogba scoring the decisive spot kick himself.

His contract was due to expire that June, and Chelsea confirmed just three days after the final that he'd be leaving. The header and winning penalty that delivered the club's first Champions League turned out to be the closing act of his first Chelsea spell, not his whole Chelsea story. He came back two years later under José Mourinho, won the league and League Cup again in 2014-15, and got a proper planned send-off the second time round, chaired off by his teammates in his actual final Chelsea appearance.

The kicker

Chelsea finished 6th in the Premier League that season, their worst league finish of the Abramovich era up to that point, though not forever, they'd go on to finish 10th in 2015-16, in the same year they became champions of Europe. Di Matteo was rewarded with the permanent manager's job that June. He was sacked five months later, in November, after a 3-0 defeat to Juventus. Counting his caretaker spell, the man who delivered Chelsea's greatest trophy lasted about eight and a half months in the job, start to finish.

Same club, both times

A club that only exists because a stadium deal fell through would, more than a hundred years later, win its greatest trophy on the back of a managerial sacking and a caretaker with nothing to lose, playing a squad Abramovich's money had built in the first place. Chelsea's history isn't only a story about money. It's also a story about what happens when things don't go to plan.
