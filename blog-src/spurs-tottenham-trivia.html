Spurs Trivia: Tottenham Used to Win Things

By Club Ten · Published 2 September 2026

If you're new to Spurs, here's the thing nobody tells you early enough: this club actually used to win stuff. Not almost. Not nearly. Won it. In May 1963, in Rotterdam, Tottenham beat Atlético Madrid 5-1 to become the first British club in history to win a European trophy. Not the first to reach a final. The first to win one, full stop. I need you to sit with that for a second, because everything else about supporting this club makes a lot more sense once you know it used to be like this.

The night nobody in Britain had done this before

Spurs went to Feijenoord Stadion and put five past a genuinely good Atlético side in the 1963 European Cup Winners' Cup final. Jimmy Greaves scored twice, John White got one, Terry Dyson got the other two. Manchester United hadn't done it yet. Liverpool hadn't done it yet. Celtic hadn't done it yet. Tottenham got there first, five years before any other British club won a European Cup of any description, and they didn't scrape through, they won 5-1.

It's a strange one to know as a Spurs fan today, because it doesn't fit the story you get told about this club. You get told about nearly. You don't get told that the club was, at one point, quite literally the best in Britain at the thing British clubs are supposed to be bad at.

And it wasn't a fluke, they'd already proven it

Two years earlier, in 1960-61, that same core of players won the league and FA Cup in the same season under Bill Nicholson, the first club to do the Double in the entire 20th century. Nobody had managed it since Aston Villa in 1896-97. They won their first 11 league games of that season, a club record that still hasn't been broken, sealed the title with a win over Sheffield Wednesday in April, then beat Leicester City 2-0 at Wembley in May.

So by the time 1963 came around, this wasn't some underdog fairy tale. It was the same group of players confirming what they'd already shown two years before: this club, for a few years in the early sixties, was simply better than everyone else in the country. Bill Nicholson stuck around until 1974, sixteen years in charge, eight major trophies along the way, still the longest and most successful managerial reign the club has ever had.

So what happened to all that?

Somewhere between then and now, "we win things" quietly turned into "we nearly get things," and nowhere is that clearer than in the transfers that didn't happen. Tell a Spurs fan you're writing about near misses and watch which stories they bring up unprompted.

Willian's is the one that still stings the most. He came in, did the medical, by most accounts the deal was done, and then somehow ended up signing for Chelsea instead. Not "the move fell through in negotiations." He'd already had the medical. Bruno Fernandes is the more painful modern one: Spurs had a fee agreed with Sporting CP in the summer of 2019, fresh off reaching a Champions League final, and pulled out over how the deal was structured. He joined Manchester United six months later and turned into one of the best midfielders in the league. Then in 2025, Tottenham thought they'd got Eberechi Eze wrapped up for around £80m, right up until Arsenal swooped in at the last moment and took him instead. Of all the clubs to lose one to.

Same club, somehow

That's the whole strange arc in one paragraph: a club that was first in Britain to win a European trophy, first to do the Double in the 20th century, run by a manager who won eight trophies in sixteen years, somehow became the club that can't get a signed player through the door without someone else swooping in. The 1963 side didn't nearly win in Rotterdam. They won. It's just taken the sixty-odd years since to make that fact feel like trivia instead of identity.
