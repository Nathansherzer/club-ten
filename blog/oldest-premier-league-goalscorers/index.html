The Oldest Goal Scorers in Premier League History at Arsenal, Chelsea, Liverpool, Man City, Man United and Spurs

7 September 2026 · 5 min read · PL era only · ranked by age at time of scoring

Scoring a Premier League goal in your mid-thirties is a feat in itself. Doing it past 36 puts you in a very small group of players whose bodies lasted longer than almost any of their peers. Here are the oldest scorers at each of the Big Six clubs since the Premier League began in 1992.

Arsenal

Dennis Bergkamp leads at 36 years and 332 days — remarkable for a player who retired from international football years earlier due to his fear of flying. Lee Dixon, a full-back finding the net at 36, sits second. Thierry Henry's goal at 34 came during his brief loan return from New York Red Bulls in 2012, the only player in this list to score as a returning loanee.

Chelsea

Thiago Silva's goal at 39 years and 198 days is one of the most extraordinary in this list — a centre-back scoring in the Premier League at an age when most players have been retired for years. Didier Drogba at 37 had already left and returned to Chelsea before scoring again. Gianfranco Zola's goal at 36 was quintessentially Zola: elegant and seemingly effortless.

Liverpool

Gary McAllister's goal at 36 came during Liverpool's extraordinary cup treble season of 2000/01 — including a famous long-range free kick against Everton in the dying minutes at Goodison. Sami Hyypiä at 35 reflects Liverpool's tradition of getting the very best out of experienced Scandinavian defenders. Mohamed Salah reaching 10th on this list at just 33 suggests he may climb it further.

Manchester City

Fernandinho leads City's list at 36 years and 362 days — a figure that reflects how long he remained a trusted part of Pep Guardiola's squad. Frank Lampard's loan spell at City in 2014/15 produced goals that famously helped knock Chelsea out of the title race. Yaya Touré at 34 and David Silva also in the thirties show just how long City's greatest era retained its core.

Manchester United

Ryan Giggs leads United's list at 39 years and 86 days — scoring Premier League goals into his fortieth year is a feat matched by almost nobody in the competition's history. Paul Scholes at 37, Cristiano Ronaldo at 37 on his return spell, and Bryan Robson at 36 complete a list that reads like a roll call of United legends.
